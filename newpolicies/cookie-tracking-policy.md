## 2. What are cookies and similar technologies?

- **Cookies** are small text files stored on your browser or device when you visit a website.
- **Local storage** and **session storage** are browser-based storage mechanisms similar to cookies.
- **SDKs**, **device identifiers** and similar technologies perform comparable functions inside mobile apps (remembering you, measuring usage and performance, maintaining security).

We refer to all of these together as "**cookies**" or "**tracking technologies**" in this Policy.

---

## 3. Where we use them

- On our **websites** (landing pages, marketing pages, legal pages and shared recipe pages).
- Within the **Just Cooked™ mobile app** (iOS and Android), via local storage and cache, SDK identifiers and pseudonymous IDs, authentication tokens, app store and subscription identifiers, and third-party SDKs (authentication, analytics, paywall management, app updates).

## 4. Why we use cookies and tracking technologies

1. **Strictly necessary / essential** – to operate the Service: keeping you logged in, managing your session, enforcing security, preventing abuse, remembering basic settings and privacy choices.
2. **Functionality** – remembering your choices (language, region, app-level preferences) so you don't re-enter them.
3. **Performance and reliability** – understanding how the Service performs and detecting issues: error and crash reports, basic performance metrics, aggregated statistics about which features are used most.
4. **Analytics and improvement** – we use **PostHog** in the mobile app for product analytics (see Section 5.4). We rely on **legitimate interests** for service improvement, debugging and performance monitoring.
5. **Marketing and advertising (not used)** – we do **not** use advertising or marketing cookies on the Just Cooked™ website or in the app, and we do not track you across other companies' apps or websites. If this ever changes, we will update this Policy, explain what is used and by whom, and obtain consent where required first.

## 5. Types of technologies we use

### 5.1 Strictly necessary / essential

Required for the Service to function and cannot be disabled without breaking core functionality:

- Session and authentication tokens used by our backend (Supabase) to verify your identity and keep you signed in
- Security mechanisms that help detect and prevent malicious activity
- App store and subscription identifiers used to verify your Premium status (RevenueCat)

### 5.2 Functionality

Remember settings and choices for convenience, for example which step or screen you were on and in-app preference choices. Disabling these reduces convenience but the Service still works.

### 5.3 Performance and reliability

Help us diagnose problems in aggregate:

- Error and crash reporting (delivered through our analytics SDK as error events, which can include technical stack traces)
- Basic performance metrics such as load times and error rates
- Aggregated usage statistics

We use this to maintain and improve the Service, not to build profiles for advertising.

### 5.4 Analytics (PostHog, mobile app)

We use **PostHog**, hosted in the **EU**, for product analytics. The SDK:

- Assigns a pseudonymous identifier to your device; when you sign in, that identifier is linked to your account so we can understand usage across sessions and devices
- Records events describing actions in the App (for example generating a recipe, opening a support tool), in some cases including names of items you create (such as a pantry item or folder name)
- Automatically captures screens viewed and taps, which can include the label of the element tapped
- Records technical information (device model, operating system, app version)
- Fetches feature flags so we can roll features out gradually
- Captures error events with technical stack traces when something goes wrong

Your analytics profile also includes account-level details described in Section 7.1 of our Privacy Policy.

We do **not** use PostHog for advertising, marketing attribution or cross-app tracking, and we do not send it your health-related information or settings, the content of your notes, photos, voice input, or the personal note in the Panic tool.

### 5.5 Subscription, paywall and update technologies

- **RevenueCat** uses an app-user identifier (your account ID) to verify purchases and entitlements.
- **Superwall** uses the same identifier to decide which paywall to present and to measure paywall performance.
- **Expo / EAS Updates** checks for app updates when the App loads, which sends device and app runtime metadata to our update infrastructure.

### 5.6 Advertising (not used)

We do not use advertising cookies or SDKs, and the App does not request the iOS App Tracking Transparency permission because we do not track you across other apps.

## 6. Managing your preferences

### 6.1 On the website (browser)

Most browsers let you view, delete and block cookies and clear local storage, usually under "Settings", "Privacy" or "Security". Blocking or deleting cookies may make some features work less reliably. Strictly necessary cookies are required for basic operation.

### 6.2 In the mobile app

You can control device-level settings through your operating system (for example app permissions and background data). Where the App offers privacy or preference settings, you can adjust them in-app. Disabling certain permissions or identifiers may reduce functionality (for example login or subscription verification).

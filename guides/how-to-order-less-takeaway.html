How to order less takeaway without the guilt

Ordering less takeaway starts with noticing when you order, not with banning it. Most takeaways happen at the same moment: tired, hungry, nothing decided. Put an easier option in that moment, something faster than the app on your phone, and the orders drop on their own.

By the Just Cooked teamUpdated 7 November 20262 min read

Try this first

  1. Spot your moment. Most people order at the same time, for the same reason. Friday at eight because the week’s over. Tuesday because nothing’s decided. Name yours.
  2. Beat the delivery time. A takeaway takes 30 to 45 minutes to arrive. Keep two meals in that take ten. Noodles with an egg. Pasta with pesto. Beans on toast.
  3. Make the easy thing visible. If the noodles are in the cupboard behind the flour, you’ll order. Put them at the front.
  4. Keep takeaway in the plan. One a week, chosen on purpose, tastes better than three ordered in a slump. Cutting to zero usually bounces back.
  5. Count what you keep. Write down what you didn’t spend. Seeing it add up is more motivating than any rule.

Why takeaway wins so often

A takeaway isn’t really about the food. It’s about not having to decide, not having to cook and not having to wash up, at the exact moment you have nothing left. The apps are built for that moment: three taps and it’s handled. Cooking has to compete with that, and on a bad night, a recipe with twelve steps loses.

So the answer isn’t to be stronger. It’s to make the home option as easy as the app.

Make cooking the easier choice

You only need to be faster than delivery. Keep two or three meals in that take ten minutes with no decisions: instant noodles with frozen veg and an egg, pasta with a jar of sauce, a quesadilla, eggs on toast. Decide them in advance, keep the ingredients in, and put a note on the fridge. When the moment comes, the choice is already made.

Keep takeaway, on purpose

Banning something usually makes it louder. A takeaway you chose, on a night you planned, is a good thing, and it costs less than the ones ordered out of nothing. Aim for fewer, not none. If you’re at four a week, two is a real win. If you’re at two, one is.

Watch what you save

Takeaways cost more than they feel like at the time. If your usual order is £20 and you swap two a week for something from the cupboard, that’s about £2,000 a year. Seeing the number grow is a far better motivator than guilt, and it turns each home-cooked meal into something you did rather than something you didn’t do.

Questions people ask

How do I stop ordering takeaway so much?
Work out when you order, then put a ten-minute meal in that exact moment. Keep the ingredients in and visible. Aim for fewer takeaways rather than none, and keep a note of what you save. Bans tend to bounce back. Easier options stick.
How much money does cooking at home save?
If a takeaway costs around £20 and you swap two a week for something from the cupboard, that’s roughly £2,000 a year. Just Cooked can track this for you: tell it your usual order and each cooked meal adds to the total.
Is it bad to get takeaway every day?
It’s expensive, and it often means skipping the meals you’d have made, but it isn’t a moral failing. Daily takeaway usually means something else is hard, like energy or deciding. Start by swapping one a week for the easiest meal you can face.

What to eat when you can’t decide

When you can’t decide what to eat, the problem is usually too many options and too little energy, not a lack of ideas. Shrink the choice to one question and start from what you already have. The first good-enough meal wins, and deciding gets easier when you stop looking for the best meal and pick the next one.

By the Just Cooked teamUpdated 7 November 20263 min read

Try this first

  1. Ask one question. Not “what do I want?” but “hot or cold?” Then “bowl or plate?” Two small answers get you most of the way there.
  2. Look before you think. Open the fridge and the cupboard and name three things you can see. A meal usually appears from those alone.
  3. Set a ten-minute limit. If it isn’t decided in ten minutes, the default wins: eggs on toast, pasta with whatever’s in, a sandwich. Defaults are not failures.
  4. Make it smaller. You don’t have to decide dinner. You only have to decide the next thing you’ll eat.
  5. Keep a short list. Three meals you can always face, written on your phone. When nothing sounds right, it’s one of those.

Why deciding feels so hard

By the time you’re hungry, you’ve already made hundreds of small decisions that day. Food arrives as one more, with no clear right answer and a tired brain to make it. Nothing sounds good because nothing is being judged fairly. That isn’t fussiness. It’s what a worn-out decision-maker does.

It gets harder when the question is too big. “What should I eat?” quietly includes what you fancy, what’s in, how long it takes, what’s healthy and what you can be bothered to wash up afterwards. No wonder it stalls.

Make the question smaller

You don’t need the best meal. You need a meal that’s good enough, soon. So swap the big question for a chain of small ones that each have an easy answer. Hot or cold. Quick or slow. Something you’ve had this week or something different. Each answer cuts the options in half, and by the third one there’s usually only one obvious thing left.

Start from what’s in, not from a blank page

A blank page is the hardest place to decide from. A fridge isn’t blank. Pick the thing that needs using up first, then ask what goes with it. Half an onion, some eggs and a bit of cheese is already an omelette. Last night’s rice is already fried rice. Working from what you have gives you a starting point, and it saves a trip to the shop you didn’t want to make anyway.

Keep a list of meals you can always face

Most people have three or four meals they never get tired of. They’re the answer on the days nothing sounds right, so write them down somewhere you’ll see them. The list doesn’t need to be impressive. Beans on toast counts. A bowl of cereal counts. The point of the list is that you never have to start from nothing.

If nothing sounds good at all

Sometimes the problem isn’t choosing. It’s that no food appeals and you’d rather skip it. On those days, aim lower. Something small and easy, eaten now, beats something perfect, eaten never. A piece of fruit, some crackers and cheese or a yoghurt is a real answer. If that’s happening most days, our guide on eating regularly when it feels hard is written for exactly that.

Questions people ask

Why can’t I decide what to eat even when I’m hungry?
Hunger makes deciding harder, not easier. Your brain has less energy to compare options, so everything looks equally fine and equally wrong. Making the question smaller, like hot or cold, or picking from a short list of meals you always like, works better than waiting to want something.
What should I eat when nothing sounds good?
Pick something small, plain and quick, and eat it now. Toast, a yoghurt, crackers and cheese or a bowl of cereal all count. Appetite often follows the first few bites. If nothing appeals most days, it’s worth mentioning to your GP.

Guides · No numbers

A food app that doesn’t count calories

You don’t need to count calories to eat well. A food app without numbers should help with the actual hard parts, like deciding what to eat and getting a meal made, and leave you calmer about food rather than watched. Just Cooked is built that way: calories, streaks and goals stay hidden unless you choose to see them.

By the Just Cooked teamUpdated 7 November 20263 min read

If tracking has been making food harder

  1. Notice what the numbers do to you. If a total makes you eat less than you’re hungry for, or skip meals to “save up”, the tracking isn’t helping.
  2. Swap the question. Instead of “how much is this?”, try “is this a meal?” A base, a protein and something fresh is a yes.
  3. Eat at regular times. Three meals and a snack or two, roughly when you’d expect them. Rhythm does the job the numbers were pretending to do.
  4. Hide the numbers rather than fighting them. Delete the tracker, or turn off the totals. Out of sight is often enough.
  5. Tell someone if it’s hard to stop. That can be a sign the tracking has become more than a habit, and it’s worth talking to a GP.

Why counting can make food feel worse

Calorie counting turns every meal into a sum. For some people that’s useful. For many, it slowly becomes the thing they think about instead of the food: eating less than they’re hungry for, feeling bad about a number, giving up when the day doesn’t add up and then eating in a way they feel worse about. The app was supposed to help and instead it’s one more source of pressure.

If that sounds familiar, you’re not failing at tracking. Tracking is failing you.

What to look for instead

  • Help with the hard part: deciding what to eat and getting a meal made. That’s where most people actually get stuck.
  • No totals, no streaks, no red: nothing that scores your day or punishes a missed one.
  • Numbers that are optional: if a protein target genuinely helps you, it should be there when you ask for it, and nowhere when you don’t.
  • Room for real life: takeaway nights, cereal dinners and days that go sideways, without a lecture.

Eating well without a tracker

Regular meals matter more than exact amounts. Aim for three meals a day and something in between if you’re hungry, built around a base, a protein and something fresh. Eat when you’re hungry, stop around when you’re full, and expect that to be imperfect. Over weeks, this gets you to a steadier place than any total does, and it leaves your head free for other things.

How Just Cooked handles numbers

Calories and protein stay hidden unless you switch them on. Gentle mode goes further and softens the whole app, quietly hiding numbers, streaks and goals so food feels lighter. If a gentle daily target is useful to you, it’s there as an estimate rather than a rule, and it’s easy to turn off again. Just Cooked isn’t a diet or calorie-counting app, and it isn’t a medical or therapy app either. It’s for the days food feels hard.

When it’s more than a hard day

If food has felt hard for a while, or you’re skipping meals most days, it’s worth telling someone. Your GP is a good first call. In the UK, Beat helps anyone who’s worried about their eating, whether or not it has a name. Samaritans are there any time on 116 123, free, for whatever is going on. NHS 111 can help when it’s urgent but not an emergency, and in an emergency you call 999. Outside the UK, a doctor and your local emergency number are the right first calls. Just Cooked isn’t a medical or therapy app, and it isn’t a substitute for professional care.

How Just Cooked helps with this

  • Numbers hidden by default. Calories and protein stay out of sight unless you switch them on. There are no streaks to lose.
  • Gentle mode. Soften the whole app whenever you want. Numbers, streaks and goals quietly disappear so food feels lighter.
  • A focus, not a score. Pick one small, doable thing to follow this week, and change it whenever. Nothing is scored.

Questions people ask

Is Just Cooked a calorie counting app?
No. Calories and protein stay hidden unless you choose to switch them on, and Gentle mode hides numbers, streaks and goals across the whole app. It’s a food app for the days food feels hard, not a diet app.
Can I eat healthily without counting calories?
Yes. Regular meals built around a base, a protein and something fresh do most of the work. Eat when you’re hungry and stop around when you’re full. It won’t be exact, and it doesn’t need to be.
What if I want a protein or calorie goal sometimes?
You can switch one on in Just Cooked. It’s shown as a gentle estimate rather than a rule, and it’s easy to turn off again when it stops being useful.

ADHD and food: eating when your brain won’t cooperate

With ADHD, the hard part of food is rarely the cooking. It’s the starting: remembering to eat, choosing what, and getting through the dozen small steps between hungry and fed. What helps is taking steps away rather than adding willpower: external reminders, a short list of default meals, food that needs no preparation, and a way to cook one step at a time.

By the Just Cooked teamUpdated 7 November 20263 min read

Try this first

  1. Put eating on the outside. Hunger cues are unreliable with ADHD, so don’t rely on them. Alarms, a routine tied to something you already do, or a meal with a person.
  2. Keep three default meals. Decided once, written down, always in stock. When you can’t choose, it’s one of these. No thinking required.
  3. Stock zero-step food. Things you can eat with no preparation at all: fruit, cheese, crackers, yoghurt, nuts, cereal bars, microwave rice, frozen meals. They are real food.
  4. Shrink the task. Don’t cook dinner. Boil water. Then see about the next step. Starting is the whole problem, so make the first step tiny.
  5. Make it visible. Food you can see gets eaten. Food in the back of a cupboard doesn’t exist. A bowl of fruit on the side beats a drawer of it.

Why food is hard with ADHD

Cooking is a chain of boring steps with no reward until the end, and that is close to the worst possible shape of task for an ADHD brain. Add unreliable hunger signals, a tendency to hyperfocus straight through lunch, trouble choosing between options, and the fact that a takeaway app is three taps, and it’s no surprise that food is one of the most common things people with ADHD say they struggle with. It isn’t laziness. It’s a task that has been designed badly for how you work.

If you take medication, it can flatten appetite during the day and bring it back in the evening. That’s worth knowing, because “eat when hungry” then means barely eating until nine o’clock. Eating by routine instead, even small amounts, works around it.

Take steps away

Every step is a place to stall, so count them and cut them. Pre-chopped and frozen veg skip the chopping. A jar of sauce skips the sauce. Eggs skip almost everything. Plates and pans that are already clean skip the thing that stops most people starting. If a meal has more than five steps, it isn’t a weeknight meal for you, and that’s a fact about the recipe rather than about you.

Borrow structure

Decisions are expensive, so make them once. A fixed breakfast. The same three lunches. A short list of dinners you rotate without thinking. It sounds dull and it is freeing, because the energy you’d spend choosing goes somewhere better. Body-doubling helps too: cooking while on the phone to someone, or at the same time as a flatmate, makes the starting easier.

The takeaway reflex

Takeaway wins because it needs no executive function at all. You don’t have to beat it, only match it. A meal that takes ten minutes and no decisions, kept in and visible, is the competition. Our guide on ordering less takeaway goes into this properly.

Be fair to yourself

Streaks, trackers and food rules tend to go badly with ADHD: a missed day feels like failure and the whole system gets abandoned. Aim for “fed, most days” rather than perfect. If your relationship with food feels like it’s become more than logistics, if you’re skipping most meals or eating in ways that frighten you, talk to your GP. Eating difficulties are more common alongside ADHD, and they can be helped.

Questions people ask

Why do I forget to eat with ADHD?
Hunger signals are easy to miss when your attention is elsewhere, hyperfocus can carry you through mealtimes, and some medications reduce appetite during the day. Eating by routine, with reminders, works better than waiting to feel hungry.
What are easy meals for ADHD?
Anything with under five steps: eggs on toast, instant noodles with frozen veg, pasta with a jar of sauce, a quesadilla, microwave rice with a tin of something. Keep three defaults in stock and decide them once, not every night.
Is Just Cooked designed for ADHD?
It isn’t built for one condition. It’s built for the days food feels hard, and a lot of what makes it work, like one question to answer, one step at a time and no streaks, happens to remove the exact places ADHD gets stuck.

Easy meals for the days you have no energy

When you have no energy, the right meal is the one that takes the least from you. Aim for something filling that needs one pan, one bowl or no cooking at all, and let it be enough. Tired days aren’t the time for a proper dinner. They’re the time for the easiest thing that will do.

By the Just Cooked teamUpdated 7 November 20263 min read

Try this tonight

  1. Lower the bar first. Tonight’s job is to eat, not to cook well. A bowl of cereal eaten is better than a stir-fry that never happens.
  2. Pick a no-cook option. Crackers and cheese. Hummus and pitta. Yoghurt with fruit. A sandwich. Tinned fish on toast. These are meals, not snacks.
  3. Or pick a one-pan option. Eggs in any form. Pasta with a jar of sauce. Beans on toast. A packet of noodles with a handful of frozen peas thrown in.
  4. Use the freezer and the microwave. A frozen meal or a jacket potato from the microwave is a perfectly good dinner. Nobody is checking.
  5. Sit down to eat it. Even for five minutes. It helps the meal count as a break, not another task.

Why cooking feels impossible when you’re tired

Cooking is a dozen small decisions and steps in a row: what, how much, where’s the pan, when is it done, and then the washing up. On a normal day you barely notice. On a day when your energy is gone, each of those steps costs something, and the whole thing starts to look like a wall. Skipping the meal feels easier, and often that’s what happens.

The way through isn’t more willpower. It’s a smaller meal with fewer steps, decided in advance.

Meals that take five minutes and almost nothing from you

  • No cooking at all: cheese and crackers, hummus and pitta, a tin of tuna with some bread, yoghurt with fruit or cereal, a sandwich with whatever’s in, a bowl of cereal.
  • One pan, under ten minutes: scrambled eggs on toast, beans on toast, instant noodles with frozen peas and an egg stirred in, pasta with pesto or a jar of sauce, a quesadilla in a dry pan.
  • Microwave only: a jacket potato with butter and cheese, porridge, a frozen meal, leftover rice with an egg cracked in and stirred through.

None of these are impressive. All of them are dinner. Keep three or four of them in stock and the tired days have an answer before they arrive.

Make the next tired day easier now

Energy comes and goes. On a day when you have some, do one thing for the version of you who won’t: buy the bread, the eggs and the frozen peas. Cook double and freeze half. Put a note on the fridge that says “noodles, eggs, toast” so you don’t have to think. Small set-ups like this are what make low-energy days survivable, and they take ten minutes when you’re feeling okay.

If the tiredness has been there a while

Everyone has flat days. If most days feel like this, or you’ve stopped eating regularly because of it, it’s worth telling someone. Low energy that doesn’t lift can have a lot of causes, and a GP can help you find out what’s going on. Eating something, even something small, usually helps in the meantime.

When it’s more than a hard day

If food has felt hard for a while, or you’re skipping meals most days, it’s worth telling someone. Your GP is a good first call. In the UK, Beat helps anyone who’s worried about their eating, whether or not it has a name. Samaritans are there any time on 116 123, free, for whatever is going on. NHS 111 can help when it’s urgent but not an emergency, and in an emergency you call 999. Outside the UK, a doctor and your local emergency number are the right first calls. Questions people ask

What can I eat when I’m too tired to cook?
Something that needs one pan or no cooking at all: eggs on toast, beans on toast, cheese and crackers, instant noodles with frozen veg, a jacket potato from the microwave, or a sandwich. Pick the easiest one and let it be enough.
Is it okay to eat cereal for dinner?
Yes. A bowl of cereal with milk is food, and on a no-energy day it’s a good answer. If it happens most nights, add something to it when you can, like a banana or some yoghurt, and plan an easy option for next time.
Why do I stop eating when I’m exhausted or low?
Tiredness and low mood both shrink your appetite and make every step of cooking feel bigger. It’s common. Eating something small and easy usually helps more than waiting until you feel like a proper meal. If it’s most days, talk to your GP.
